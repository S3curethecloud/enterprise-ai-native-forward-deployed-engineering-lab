## 2. Job-Description Connection

Critical thinking, ambiguity reduction, knowledge sharing, reusable playbooks, and shaping how enterprises adopt AI-native engineering.

## 3. Plain-English Explanation

Translate a broad role or client objective into an ordered engineering roadmap with explicit capabilities, risks, evidence, and authority gates.

## 4. Why Enterprises Care

- AI programs fail when a model demo begins before the workflow and decision rights are understood.
- A shared capability map aligns business, architecture, engineering, security, and operations.
- Evidence gates prevent unsupported claims and premature autonomy.
- A reusable roadmap turns one project into an organizational playbook.

## 5. Reference Workflow

`JD or client objective → capability decomposition → architecture layers → risk and authority boundaries → evidence requirements → phased roadmap → exit gates`

## 6. Architecture and Delivery Decisions

- Separate learning scope, implementation authority, and production authority.
- Require earlier ambiguity to be resolved before later technology expands scope.
- Map every JD responsibility to an artifact, test, or future gate.
- Keep portfolio evidence distinct from external client-delivery claims.

## 7. Thirty-Second Core Answer

I start ambiguous AI-native work by decomposing the objective into capabilities, decision rights, risks, evidence, and release gates. In this lab I translated the role into an 18-phase roadmap covering discovery, orchestration, RAG, providers, tools, policy, evaluation, observability, deployment, and reuse. That prevents a team from jumping directly to an agent demo without defining what must be secure, measurable, and operational.

## 8. Sixty-Second Core Answer

My first step in an ambiguous engagement is to turn broad language into an engineering decision system. I identify the user workflow, business outcome, agent responsibilities, deterministic authorities, integration boundaries, risks, evidence requirements, and phase gates. In the portfolio lab, I mapped the AI Native Engineer role into 18 top-level phases and linked each phase to artifacts and exit criteria. The important discipline is that later technology cannot compensate for an unresolved earlier decision—for example, a sophisticated model cannot repair unclear authority or missing success metrics. This is how I would embed with a client: establish shared language, expose assumptions, sequence delivery, and create a playbook the business and engineering teams can inspect together.

## 11. Claim Defense

Verified independent portfolio work: JD map, 18-phase roadmap, governance rules, evidence model, and phase gates. Do not describe it as an Accenture artifact or external client deliverable.

When challenged, identify:

- Your exact role
- Whether the setting was client-facing, professional internal work, or portfolio work
- Stakeholders involved
- Artifacts you personally produced
- What was designed versus implemented
- Local, CI, prototype, pilot, or production status
- Measurable evidence
- Known limitations
